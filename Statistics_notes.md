# Statistics Notes

## 1. Statistics

Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data to make decisions or draw conclusions.

**i) Descriptive Statistics** — Summarizes and presents data using tables, graphs, and numerical measures.
*Example:* The average score of a class is 82%.

**ii) Inferential Statistics** — Uses sample data to make conclusions or predictions about a larger population.
*Example:* Polling 1,000 voters to estimate how all voters in a country will vote.

## 2. Population

The population is the entire group of individuals or items of interest in a study.
*Example:* All students at a university.

## 3. Sample

A sample is a subset of the population selected for study.
*Example:* 200 students chosen from the university.

## 4. Variable

A variable is a characteristic that can take different values for different individuals.
*Example:* Height, age, or eye color.

## 5. Data

Data are the observed values collected for variables.

- **Datum** (singular): One observation. *Example:* One student's age is 20.
- **Data** (plural): Multiple observations. *Example:* Ages of 200 students.

## 6. Experiment

An experiment is a study in which researchers impose a treatment or condition and observe its effects.
*Example:* Testing whether a new fertilizer increases plant growth.

## 7. Parameter

A parameter is a numerical measure that describes a population.
*Example:* The average age of all students at a university.

## 8. Statistic

A statistic is a numerical measure calculated from a sample.
*Example:* The average age of the 200 sampled students.

## Types of Data

**1. Categorical Data** — Consists of labels or categories rather than numerical measurements.
*Example:* Gender, blood type, favorite color, car brand.

**2. Numerical Data** — Consists of numbers that represent counts or measurements.

- **a) Discrete Data** — Countable values, usually whole numbers.
  *Example:* Number of children in a family, number of cars owned, number of defects in a product.
- **b) Continuous Data** — Can take any value within a range and are usually obtained by measurement.
  *Examples:* Height (170.5 cm), weight (68.2 kg), temperature (22.7°C), time (3.45 seconds).

## Levels of Measurement

**i) Qualitative** — A variable that categorizes or describes a population element.

**ii) Quantitative** — A variable that quantifies a population element (Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, Ratio).

**1. Nominal Data** — Consists of categories with no natural order or ranking.
*Example:* Nationality, blood type.

**2. Ordinal Data** — Consists of categories that can be ranked, but the differences between ranks are not necessarily equal.
*Example:* Class rank, education level.

**3. Interval Data** — Numerical data with equal intervals between values, but there is no true zero.
*Example:* Temperature in °C or °F.

**4. Ratio Data** — Numerical data with equal intervals and a true zero point.
*Example:* Height, age, income.

## Measures of Central Tendency

**1. Mean** — The mean (average) is the sum of all values divided by the number of values.

**2. Median** — The middle value when data are arranged in order.
*Example:* 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 → Median = 7

**3. Mode** — The value that occurs most frequently.
*Example:* 2, 3, 3, 4, 5 → Mode = 3

## Measures of Dispersion

**1. Range** — The difference between the largest and smallest values.

**2. Variance** — Measures the average squared deviation of data values from the mean.
*Example:* If scores are very spread out, the variance is large; if scores are clustered near the mean, the variance is small.

**3. Standard Deviation** — The square root of the variance. It measures the typical distance of data values from the mean.
*Example:* If the mean test score is 80 and the standard deviation is 5, most scores tend to be within about 5 points of 80.

## Distribution Shape

**1. Symmetrical Distribution** — Data evenly distributed around the center. The left and right sides are approximately mirror images. Mean = Median = Mode.

**2. Asymmetrical Distribution** — Not balanced around its center. One side extends farther than the other. The distribution is skewed either left or right.

**3. Right-Skewed Distribution** — A long tail extending to the right (toward larger values).

**4. Left-Skewed Distribution** — A long tail extending to the left (toward smaller values).

**5. Bell Curve** — A perfectly symmetrical, bell-shaped distribution known as the normal distribution.

## Distributions

**1. Normal Distribution** — A continuous, bell-shaped, symmetric distribution where most values cluster around the mean.

**2. Bernoulli Distribution** — Describes a single experiment with only two possible outcomes: success or failure.
*Example:* Pass/fail.

**3. Binomial Distribution** — Describes the number of successes in a fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials.
*Example:* Number of heads in 10 coin tosses.

**4. Uniform Distribution** — All outcomes are equally likely.
*Example:* Chances of getting 1 when a die is rolled (1/6).

**5. Poisson Distribution** — Models the number of times an event occurs in a fixed interval of time, area, distance, or volume.
*Example:* Number of phone calls received per hour.

## Empirical Rule & Standardization

**1) Empirical Rule (68–95–99.7 Rule)**

For a normal distribution:

- **68%** of observations lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean.
- **95%** lie within 2 standard deviations of the mean.
- **99.7%** lie within 3 standard deviations of the mean.

**2) Standard Normal Distribution**

The standard normal distribution is a special normal distribution with:

- Mean (μ) = 0
- Standard deviation (σ) = 1

A **z-score** indicates how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean.
*Example:* A z-score of +2 means a value is 2 standard deviations above the mean.

**3) Standardization**

Standardization is the process of converting a normal random variable *X* into a standard normal variable *Z*.
